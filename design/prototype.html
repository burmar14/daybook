Daybook —
a year you can see

One column per month, one square per day. Tap a square to log it; the colour is the record. No account, no sync, no server — the year lives on the phone that drew it. Everything below is live: tap the squares, switch categories, open a day.

One tap = your usual day

Each category defines what a single tap means. Drinks logs a zero — a clear day is the common case, and it deserves the cheapest gesture. Move logs a solid session.

Empty ≠ zero

An outlined square is a day you didn't log. A green square is a day you logged as zero. Most trackers blur these two; keeping them apart is what makes the streak count honest.

Hold for the detail

Press and hold any square for the exact number, a note, and a way to clear the day. The grid stays fast; the sheet holds the nuance.

Year reads, month writes

The year grid is for seeing shape — the drift, the good stretch, the bad fortnight. Tap a month letter for a full-size calendar with comfortable targets.

Colour direction is per category

Less is better for Drinks; more is better for Move. Rather than force one scale, each category owns its ramp and the legend travels with the tab.

Rest is not failure

Move logs a rest day as a calm grey, not an alarm. A tracker that punishes recovery gets deleted in March.

The colour grammar

Six ramps. Chrome stays near-neutral so the data carries every saturated colour on the screen. Drinks is exactly the scale you asked for; the other five follow its logic.

Calls I made, and what I'd change

Where the brief left room, here is what I chose and why — plus the three things worth deciding before a line of app code gets written.

Kept

Squares that don't exist stay missing

February stops at 28; the short months notch the bottom of the grid exactly like your reference sheet. It's a small thing that makes the chart read as a real calendar instead of a heat map.

Kept

Wrapping past the top clears the day

Tap past the last level and the square returns to unlogged. It's the only way to undo a mis-tap without opening the sheet — and it means every square is reachable with one finger.

Added

A streak that counts the right thing

Each category defines its own "good day", so the streak means something different per tab: zero drinks, any movement, six thousand steps, a note written. One number, honest in six contexts.

Added

Distinct marks for red–green safety

Green-versus-red is the exact pair eight percent of men can't separate. A settings toggle puts a small dot on the two alert levels so the scale survives without colour. Worth building in week one, not month six.

Decide

The name

Daybook is a working title — it's the old word for a journal of daily entries. "Check In" is close to unsearchable on the App Store and likely collides with existing marks. Worth settling before the developer account is opened.

Decide

Backups, since there's no login

No account means a lost phone is a lost year. I'd ship an export to a file plus opt-in iCloud private sync — still no login, still no server of ours, but the data survives a new phone.
